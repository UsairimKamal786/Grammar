Day 17 Grammar: Modal Verbs Modal verbs are special verbs that show ability, possibility, permission, obligation, or advice. They are always followed by the base form of a verb. 1. Common Modal Verbs & Uses Modal Use Example can Ability / Permission I can swim. / You can go now. could Past ability / Polite request / Possibility She could read at 5. / Could you help me? may Permission / Possibility You may leave. / It may rain later. might Slight possibility He might come to the party. must Obligation / Strong certainty You must wear a helmet. / That must be true. shall Suggestion / Formal future Shall we dance? / I shall return. should Advice / Expectation You should eat healthy. / She should be home by now. will Future / Willingness I will help you. would Polite request / Hypothetical I would like a coffee. / If I were rich, I would travel. 2. Quick Tips No “s” in third person: ❌ He cans swim. → ✅ He can swim. Modal + base verb only (never “to” except “ought to”): ❌ She must to go. → ✅ She must go. Negatives: Add not after the modal: I cannot / can’t swim. You should not / shouldn’t lie. Questions: Modal + subject + base verb: Can you help me? Should we leave now? 3. Mini Exercise Fill in the blanks with the correct modal verb: You ___ study for the test if you want to pass. I ___ ride a bike when I was 5. ___ I open the window? She ___ be at school now. (I’m almost sure) We ___ leave early to catch the train. (advice)
